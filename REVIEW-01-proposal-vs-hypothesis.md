# REVIEW-01 — the proposal against the original hypothesis

Status: **review only.** No protocol, threshold, fixture, model list or decision
record was changed to produce it. The seven decisions from
`DECISION-MODEL-PROPOSAL-01.md` §9 are **held**, not answered.

This review exists because the proposal I wrote may have quietly changed what
counts as success. That is the one failure a proposal cannot be allowed to make,
so it is checked here against the hypothesis as stated, not against the
architecture it produced.

---

## 1. The hypothesis, made falsifiable

As stated: *can an inexpensive decision model ingest an extremely large body of
material in one pass and return a reliable, evidence-addressed conceptual snapshot
that preserves global context well enough to support later differential
assessment?*

Split into three separable propositions, each with its own kill criterion:

| | proposition | falsified by |
|---|---|---|
| **P1 capability** | given a comprehensive inventory of questions about a large corpus, the model answers accurately, abstains honestly, and locates its evidence | accuracy or calibration near chance, systematic silence, or unverifiable citations |
| **P2 economics** | that answering costs a small fraction of a generative model reading the same corpus | measured cost per *usable* snapshot ≥ the generative cost at equal accuracy |
| **P3 differential** | consecutive snapshots differ only where the material differs | deltas with unchanged evidence, or instability across identical input beyond what the interface forces |

The hypothesis is **not** "can it write a good summary". P1–P3 are answerable
without another generative model, which is why the cohort is viable at all.

## 2. The central finding: the interface pre-empts part of the hypothesis

A closed-set decision model can only answer what it is asked. It **cannot
volunteer an unqueried relationship.** So for this cohort:

> "conceptual completeness" is not observable from the model's output at all. What
> is observable is *conditional accuracy over a bank* — plus whatever the bank's
> own coverage of the material happens to be.

My proposal (§D2, §D3) treated the assembled snapshot as the object of assessment,
which silently converts the hypothesis into "can it answer a comprehensive bank
correctly and cheaply". That is a real and valuable question — it is not the
question as written, because it moves the decision about *what matters in the
corpus* from the model to the bank author.

Three ways to resolve this honestly, and the experiment must pick one *before*
any code:

- **R-a Neutral inventory.** The bank is generated by a rule-based or
  schema-driven scan of the corpus (section map, declared cross-references,
  entity mentions), not by a model that knows the answer. Coverage of the corpus
  is then measured independently of the model, and P1 becomes testable as written.
- **R-b Hybrid two-tier.** A thin open-ended pass (which may be a generative
  model or a cheap extractor) proposes candidate structure; the decision model
  verifies each candidate. This restores "decide what matters" but breaks the
  single-model, single-pass economics of P2 — it must then be reported as a
  two-tier cost, not one.
- **R-c Restated hypothesis.** Keep the closed-set design and restate P1 as
  *conditional* accuracy. Cheapest, and the only option that does not add an
  instrument — but it changes the research question and must be recorded as a
  deliberate revision, with the §2 loss stated in the result.

What must not happen is (R-c) adopted implicitly by an architecture that never
mentions the loss. That is what the proposal as written would have done.

## 3. Where the proposal redefines success

| # | shift in the proposal | why it is a redefinition | remedy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "snapshot" means an object assembled from answers | the model no longer decides what to report; completeness becomes a property of the bank | R-a / R-b / R-c, decided explicitly |
| 2 | coverage is measured against the bank | a bank author who asks only easy questions scores 100 % | measure `bank_coverage` against a **model-independent** structure inventory, and publish both |
| 3 | claim text is harness-rendered (§D3) | any judgement of snapshot *quality* is partly a judgement of the bank's phrasing | state it as a ceiling; never grade claim fluency as model output |
| 4 | "one pass" means one request | internal billing multiplies the state by the question count; "single pass" is a billing fiction for decisions | report billed tokens and state tokens separately, plus the question multiplier |
| 5 | cost per snapshot | a cheap model at 60 % coverage is a different product, not a cheaper one | report cost at matched accuracy (iso-accuracy), not cost alone |
| 6 | frozen thresholds reused | `required_coverage_min 0.90` was pre-registered for generative snapshots, not bank coverage | new pre-registration before the first decision run; do not reuse |

Item 6 is a category error waiting to happen, and it is invisible unless named:
the same number means two different things on the two paths.

## 4. Two axes, two instruments, never one number

The distinction to preserve: **decision accuracy is conditional; conceptual
completeness is unconditional.** They fail differently and are gameable
differently.

### Axis A — decision accuracy (given the question)

Instrument: the bank + the answers. Metrics, all conditional on being asked:

- per-question correctness (choice/score/noul against declared truth)
- calibration: Brier score and reliability bins on `noul` probabilities — the one
  capability only this cohort offers
- citation authenticity: `locate` spans that verify against the state
- abstention: low probability or empty spans where nothing supports an answer
- consistency across repeats, with the interface's non-determinism stated
- cost and latency per answered snapshot

### Axis B — conceptual completeness (regardless of what was asked)

Instrument: a **model-independent structure inventory** of the corpus —
relationships, dependencies, contradictions, gaps, and the questions that would
settle them — plus the assembled snapshot compared against it:

- `structure_coverage` = inventory entries represented anywhere in the snapshot ÷ all entries
- `bank_coverage` = inventory entries with at least one bank question ÷ all entries
  — **a property of the instrument, not of the model**
- `unqueried_entries` = the entries in neither: for a closed-set cohort this is
  structurally unanswerable, and reporting it as a model failure would be dishonest
- `silence_rate` and `answer_yield`: how often the model declines or affirms
- `answer_balance`: yes-rate on known-true items minus yes-rate on known-false
  items. An all-yes or all-no model is accurate on unbalanced banks, cheap, and
  consistent — the degenerate strategy that would otherwise win the economics

### The two failure modes, kept apart

| failure | axis | how it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| answers every question correctly, misses an unqueried relationship | B | `unqueried_entries > 0` while Axis A is high — a *bank* defect, reported as such, not as model error |
| comprehensive-looking snapshot from incomplete or misleading answers | A ∩ B | high `bank_coverage`, high `structure_coverage`, but low `answer_balance` or high `silence_rate` — the snapshot's shape is fine and its content is hollow |

A combined score would hide exactly these two rows. The report must present A and
B as separate columns and must not let a good A license any claim about B.

## 5. Amendments this review requires of the proposal

- **R1** Add §2's choice (R-a / R-b / R-c) as decision 0, ahead of the other seven.
  Until it is made, no bank, adapter or assembler should be built.
- **R2** Add a model-independent structure inventory as a required artifact, and
  report `bank_coverage` beside `structure_coverage` in every run.
- **R3** Require balanced banks: items with true answers, items with false
  answers, trap evidence, and items where the corpus supports nothing. Without
  balance the accuracy and cost results are jointly meaningless.
- **R4** Cap the claim: with harness-rendered claims, a snapshot may not be graded
  on phrasing, completeness of prose, or "conceptuality" of its language.
- **R5** New pre-registration for the decision path. The existing thresholds apply
  to the generative path only; reusing them would compare two different quantities
  under one name.
- **R6** Add the iso-accuracy view to the cost table: cost per snapshot at a fixed
  accuracy band, so a cheap-and-wrong model cannot be presented as cheap.

## 6. What the experiment can honestly say, by option

| option | result may say | result may not say |
|---|---|---|
| R-a | "given a neutral inventory, the model answered X % at $Y" | "the model understands the corpus" |
| R-b | "inventory + verification cost $Y at X % accuracy" | "single-pass economics" |
| R-c | "conditional accuracy at scale and cost" | "conceptual snapshot", "comprehensive", "global context preserved" |

Every option keeps P2 and P3 testable. Only R-a and R-b keep P1 as written; R-c
narrows it and must say so in the title of the result, not a footnote.

The draft adapter encodes three of the assumptions this review contests: that the
assembled object is the snapshot, that harness-rendered claims are acceptable, and
that a missing `locate` capability may fall back to `inferred`. It should be
treated as a sketch pending R1, not as work in progress.

Reverting it is one command and loses nothing that is not already described in
`DECISION-MODEL-PROPOSAL-01.md`.

## 8. Decisions — held, plus what the review adds

Held, unchanged: the seven in `DECISION-MODEL-PROPOSAL-01.md` §9. None is answered
here, and none should be answered before the following are settled:

- **D0** which of R-a, R-b or R-c defines the experiment — this precedes the cohort
  decision, because it decides whether "conceptual completeness" is measurable at all
- **D0a** whether a balanced-bank requirement (R3) is accepted before any fixture work
- **D0b** whether the decision path gets its own pre-registration (R5) rather than
  inheriting numbers meant for a different quantity

If D0 is R-c, then the honest title of the whole effort changes, and the proposal's
"decision model as evidence provider" framing is the right one. If D0 is R-a or
R-b, the proposal needs a second instrument before a single bank is authored.
